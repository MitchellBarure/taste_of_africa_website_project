Isombe

  • East Africa
  • Serves [4]
  • [2 hr]
  • [Easy]

Cassava leaves cooked down slowly and finished with peanut butter: one of Rwanda's best-loved stews.

The story

Isombe is a popular stew in Rwanda and is also eaten in Burundi. It is believed to have started in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. The base is cassava leaves, grown across Rwanda, cooked with tomatoes, onion and garlic, then finished with peanut butter for richness. It is eaten with ugali, rice and beans, or bread.

Ingredients

  • [1 kg] cassava leaves, fresh or frozen
  • [2] tomatoes, chopped
  • [1] onion, chopped
  • [3 cloves] garlic, crushed
  • [1 handful] coriander, chopped
  • [2 cups] stock made from meat bones
  • [4 tbsp] peanut butter
  • Salt to taste

Method

1 Prepare the leaves

Pound or finely chop the cassava leaves.

2 Boil with the vegetables

Put the leaves in a pot with the tomatoes, onion, garlic, coriander and stock. Bring to the boil.

3 Simmer until soft

Cover and cook on low heat for at least an hour, until the leaves are dark and soft. Add water if the pot gets dry. Cassava leaves must be fully cooked before eating.

4 Stir in the peanut butter

Add the peanut butter last, stir until smooth and simmer for a few more minutes. Season with salt and serve.

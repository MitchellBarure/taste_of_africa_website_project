Groundnut soup

  • West Africa
  • Serves [8]
  • [2 hr]
  • [Medium]

A rich, peppery soup of toasted ground peanuts, eaten across Nigeria from family lunches to weddings.

The story

Groundnut soup, also called peanut stew, comes from northern Nigeria, where it is known as miyar gyada. Today it is cooked across the country and served at weddings and other big occasions. Its body comes from toasted, ground peanuts, with heat from scotch bonnet and colour from palm oil. It is eaten with rice or a swallow such as fufu or eba.

Ingredients

For the meat

  • [2.5 lb] goat meat for stewing
  • [1] red onion, sliced
  • [2 tsp] bouillon
  • [1 tsp] salt

For the soup

  • [2 cups] raw skinned peanuts
  • [3] scotch bonnet peppers, chopped
  • [0.5 lb] spinach, chopped
  • [2 tbsp] ground crayfish
  • [3 tbsp] palm oil
  • Salt to taste

Method

1 Braise the goat

Cook the goat with the onion, bouillon and salt over low to medium heat for 30 to 35 minutes. Keep the liquid.

2 Toast and grind the peanuts

Toast the peanuts in a dry pan over low heat, stirring, for 15 to 25 minutes. Let them cool, then grind to a rough powder.

3 Cook the peanut base

Top up the braising liquid with water to about 3 cups. Stir in the ground peanuts and peppers until smooth, then boil gently for 10 minutes, stirring. Add water if it gets too thick.

4 Add the meat, oil and crayfish

Stir in the palm oil, braised goat and crayfish. Cook for 5 more minutes.

5 Finish with spinach

Add the spinach and simmer on low for 5 minutes, stirring. Season with salt and serve.

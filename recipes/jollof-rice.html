Jollof rice

  • West Africa
  • Serves 6
  • 1 hr 15 min
  • Medium

One pot, a smoky tomato base and a friendly rivalry: Nigeria and Ghana have argued for years over who makes it best.

The story

The dish is widely linked to the Wolof (Jolof) people of the Senegambia region. Today it is party food across West Africa, built on a blended base of tomatoes, red peppers and scotch bonnet. Many cooks swear the best part is the smoky bottom layer of the pot.

Ingredients

6
  • 3 cups long-grain parboiled rice
  • 4 ripe plum tomatoes
  • 2 red bell peppers
  • 1 scotch bonnet pepper
  • 2 onions
  • 3 tbsp tomato paste
  • 3 cups chicken or vegetable stock
  • 2 bay leaves, 1 tsp thyme, 1 tsp curry powder
  • ½ cup vegetable oil, salt to taste

Method

1 Blend the pepper base

Blend the tomatoes, peppers, scotch bonnet and one onion until smooth. Boil it down until thick and most of the water is gone.

2 Fry the tomato paste

Heat the oil, soften the second onion, sliced, then fry the tomato paste for a few minutes until it darkens. Stir in the reduced pepper base.

3 Add stock, spices and rice

Add the stock, bay leaves, thyme, curry powder and salt. Rinse the rice, stir it in and make sure the liquid just covers it.

4 Steam low and slow

Cover tightly and cook on low heat until the rice is tender. Leave it a few minutes at the end to build the smoky bottom layer.
